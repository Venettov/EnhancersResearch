What the result means

The paper-normalized model correctly classified 2,270 of 2,968 sequences; the saved iLearn model classified 2,280 correctly. The conditional paired 95% interval for the difference is -0.85 to +0.17 percentage points. The paired interval includes zero, so this run does not establish a clear accuracy gain or loss. It does not prove equivalence.

Mean fold AUROC was 0.8475 with iLearn normalization and 0.8481 with the paper denominator. AUROC describes ranking; differences are descriptive, without a specified uncertainty interval.

Why the two formulas differ

A 200-base sequence contains 198 overlapping three-letter windows. iLearn divides each word’s count by 198, so the 64 word frequencies sum to 1. The paper’s Equation 7 defines the denominator as sequence length: 200. Those frequencies sum to 0.99. For example, a word occurring 10 times has values 10/198 ≈ 0.050505 and 10/200 = 0.05.

Every sequence has the same length, so the paper formula is a uniform 1% reduction of this block, not additional sequence information. The 64 word columns retain their ordering across records; all 15,153 other columns are exactly unchanged. Total dimensionality remains 15,217.

Order-based tree splits and per-feature standardization can be invariant to a positive constant rescaling in exact arithmetic. Our training-only standardization check found agreement within numerical precision for all 20 outer/inner partitions. Finite-precision training can still change a fitted model; the measured probability and decision differences are recorded below. This result cannot establish the authors’ actual normalization choice.

Follow-up check: where did the change arise?

All five tree-based classifiers had exactly identical held-out probabilities with both normalizations. Their out-of-fold training probabilities also matched exactly. The neural network changed 30 held-out class decisions, and the combined model changed 56. The neural network’s accuracy rose from 73.08% to 73.42%, while the combined model’s accuracy fell: improving one component does not guarantee an improvement in their combination.

To check repeatability, a separately recorded post-result diagnostic repeated the full-training neural-network fit twice per normalization on outer fold 1 (internal fold 0). That fold was chosen by a fixed first-fold rule, not because it had the largest change. All four additional fits completed without warnings. Both repetitions reproduced their own saved benchmark probabilities exactly, for all 594 held-out records. The difference between the two normalizations persisted. This argues against ordinary rerun variation for this fold and is consistent with sensitivity of neural-network optimization to tiny numerical input differences.

Standardized features agree within numerical precision, but they are not guaranteed to be bit-identical. The specific low-level optimization path has not been isolated. This four-fit probe covers one fold and does not establish general stability or alter the primary 125-fit benchmark or its uncertainty interval.

What remains unresolved

This is an exploratory repaired reconstruction on previously examined historical enhancer/non-enhancer labels. It does not test strong versus weak enhancers or cell-specific activity. Published settings may already have used these labels. Bootstrap intervals condition on saved predictions and omit refitting, seed variation and prior-selection uncertainty.

The contaminated historical test and all NEW_2026_DATA outcomes were excluded. Feature-family mapping, final ensemble choices and the historical software environment remain unresolved. This denominator test does not reproduce the complete paper or resolve the separate lentiMPRA specificity-score normalization problem. A stronger confirmation needs an independently specified evaluation.
