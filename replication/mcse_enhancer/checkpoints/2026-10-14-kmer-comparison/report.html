What the result means

The 3-mer version classified 2,280 of 2,968 sequences correctly, compared with 2,248 for 2-mers. The paired 95% interval for the accuracy difference is +0.24 to +1.91 percentage points. The interval is above zero, supporting a modest accuracy gain in this fixed comparison. These previously explored folds do not provide fresh independent confirmation.

A 2-mer is a two-letter DNA word such as AC; a 3-mer is a three-letter word such as ACG. Three-letter words describe more specific local patterns, but more detail does not automatically improve predictions. This comparison measures the effect of that representation change under the fixed pipeline; it cannot identify a biological mechanism.

Accuracy measures correct enhancer/non-enhancer labels. AUROC measures how well scores rank the classes; 0.5 is chance ranking and 1.0 is perfect. Mean fold AUROC was 0.8471 for 2-mers and 0.8475 for 3-mers. The observed ranking difference is small relative to the change in classification accuracy at the fixed 0.5 threshold. Ranking differences are descriptive; no uncertainty interval for them was specified.

What changed—and what was held fixed

The 16-column Kmer2 frequency block was replaced with the 64-column Kmer3 block. All 15,153 common features were checked for exact equality. Independent overlapping-window counts verified both word blocks for every training sequence. For these 200-base sequences, frequencies use 199 windows for 2-mers and 198 for 3-mers. The paper instead writes a denominator of sequence length; that separate ambiguity was not mixed into this comparison.

The other descriptor families already include some 3-mer information. This experiment changes only the separate Kmer block; it does not compare all possible sources of 2-mer versus 3-mer information.

The same 2,968 rows, labels, 2,451 groups, five outer folds, three inner cross-fitting folds, six base classifiers, CatBoost final model, S1 settings, MLP training-only scaling, random seed, software and 0.5 threshold were retained. No parameter search or selection of a winning model was performed. The original 2-mer predictions were verified and reused, not refitted.

Which decisions changed?

Both versions were correct on 2,182 sequences and wrong on 622. The 3-mer version corrected 98 errors from the 2-mer version, while introducing 66 errors on records the 2-mer version classified correctly. The change in accuracy is the net of these changes, not the total number of changed decisions.

Validation and limits

All 125 new fits and 125 saved control fits were audited for group/record separation and complete prediction coverage. The historical contaminated 400-record test and all new lentiMPRA outcomes were excluded. Sources, inputs, effective settings, runtime versions and feature identities were checked. New fit warnings: 0.

The paired intervals use 2,000 whole-group resamples within the fixed outer folds, seed 20261014. They are conditional on fitted predictions and omit refitting, seed variation and parameter-selection uncertainty. Published S1 settings may already have been selected using these benchmark labels; this follows earlier inspection of the same folds. No fresh independent confirmation is claimed.

Changing feature dimensions can change model sampling and training trajectories even with a fixed seed. The result concerns this pipeline as a whole. It does not establish the exact published matrix, optimal word length, or transfer to cell-specific experimental activity. No final deployment model or serialized estimator weights were created.
