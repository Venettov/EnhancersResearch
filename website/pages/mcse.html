How to read the results

The same benchmark, with related sequences kept together

The original data contain 2,968 sequences: 742 strong enhancers, 742 weak enhancers and 1,484 non-enhancers. Related sequences were assigned to 2,451 groups that stayed together across five outer evaluation folds. Model selection used training data within each fold.

The separate historical 400-record test contains 100 exact training overlaps and was excluded from these evaluations. The comparisons below reuse the grouped benchmark, so they are exploratory rather than new independent confirmations. A reported uncertainty interval describes its stated analysis; it does not remove the limits of reused data.

The repaired MCSE-Enhancer implementation correctly classified about 76 of every 100 DNA sequences as enhancers or non-enhancers. It is now a working, checked comparison model. This first run does not establish an accuracy improvement over our earlier iEnhancer-2L binary classifier.

Accuracy is the fraction of sequences assigned the correct label. AUROC measures how well the scores rank enhancers above non-enhancers: 0.5 is chance-level ranking and 1.0 is perfect. Here it is averaged across folds, which are portions of data held aside during training. The small ranking difference is descriptive; no uncertainty analysis for that difference was specified.

What the result means in simple terms

  • The methods are close on this benchmark. MCSE’s accuracy was 0.47 percentage points lower. The paired 95% uncertainty interval for MCSE minus the SVM is −1.69 to +0.67 points. It includes zero, so these predictions do not establish a clear accuracy advantage for either method. This does not prove that the methods are equivalent.
  • More classifiers do not automatically make a better predictor. MCSE combines six models through a final model that learns how to use their predictions. This particular combination did not clearly improve accuracy. The two methods also use different features and parameter-selection procedures, so the comparison cannot isolate the effect of combining models.
  • This is a computational result, not a new biological discovery. The task was enhancer versus non-enhancer classification using historical labels. It did not test strong versus weak enhancers, activity in particular cells, or the new lentiMPRA measurements.

What was repaired?

The inspected notebooks had incomplete cells, a cross-validation problem that used only the final split, and optimization using the supposed test labels. Our implementation checks the recovered feature calculations, evaluates all five outer folds, and trains the combined model using out-of-fold predictions: each example’s training prediction comes from a base model that excluded its group. The neural network’s scaling is fitted within training partitions. The contaminated historical 400-record test is excluded entirely.

Repairing the evaluation does not recover every historical choice. The reported Table S1 settings may already have been selected using this benchmark, and these folds have informed our earlier exploratory analyses. This is a diagnostic run with reported settings, not a fresh independent confirmation. The paper’s historical 81.5% accuracy comes from a different evaluation and is not a directly comparable target.

Data, recovered settings and remaining choices

The run used 1,484 enhancers and 1,484 non-enhancers, organized into 2,451 sequence/locus groups. Sequences assigned to the same group stayed together across five outer folds and three inner cross-fitting folds. Each of the 2,968 records received one held-out prediction. No hyperparameter search or threshold optimization was performed during this run.

The notebook_k2_full variant was chosen before viewing MCSE performance. It contains 15,169 features and follows the notebook’s 2-mer command and full numerical/count block. The paper describes 3-mers, and the mapping of feature blocks remains unresolved. The six base classifiers are random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, Extra Trees and a neural network (MLP). A fixed CatBoost model combines their positive-class probabilities, without raw-feature passthrough. Reusing the reported CatBoost base settings for this final model is our declared choice; the paper’s exact final settings were not recovered.

The isolated run used Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1, LightGBM 4.7.0, XGBoost 3.4.1 and CatBoost 1.2.10. The random seed was 20261014, with one CPU thread per fit and a 2,000-iteration cap for the neural network. These are recorded runtime choices, not recovered author software versions.

MCSE-Enhancer · 2-mer versus 3-mer follow-up · 14 October 2026

Three-letter DNA words gave a modest accuracy gain

Changing one group of sequence features from two-letter to three-letter DNA words increased accuracy from 75.74% to 76.82%: 32 more correct classifications among the same 2,968 sequences. This comparison asks whether a different way of describing DNA helps identify enhancers; it does not measure enhancer strength or activity in particular cells.

Repaired reconstruction · exploratory result. Both versions use our repaired MCSE-Enhancer evaluation. The 3-mer version follows the paper’s stated word length, but unresolved feature definitions, normalization and ensemble choices mean that neither version is an exact reproduction of the published study.

What are 2-mers and 3-mers?

DNA uses four letters: A, C, G and T. A 2-mer is a two-letter word, such as AC; a 3-mer has three letters, such as ACG. For the short sequence ACGT, overlapping 2-mers are AC, CG, GT, while 3-mers are ACG, CGT. The model receives a numerical summary of how often each word occurs. Longer words describe a longer local pattern, but that alone does not guarantee better predictions.

What the result means in simple terms

  • A small net improvement, with tradeoffs. The 3-mer version corrected 98 mistakes made by the 2-mer version, but introduced 66 new mistakes: 32 additional correct answers overall. Accuracy improved in four of the five folds.
  • Correct labels improved more than overall ranking. Accuracy measures correct decisions at the fixed 0.5 probability threshold. AUROC measures how well scores rank enhancers above non-enhancers; its mean across folds barely changed, from 0.8471 to 0.8475. That ranking comparison is descriptive, without a specified uncertainty test for the AUROC difference.
  • This does not show why a particular DNA word matters biologically. Only the standalone word-frequency block changed; other blocks already contain some 3-mer information. Changing the number of features can also change model training and feature sampling, even with the same random seed.
What changed, what stayed fixed, and what remains unresolved

The 2-mer block has 16 possible words; the 3-mer block has 64. Total feature counts changed from 15,169 to 15,217. All 15,153 shared feature columns were identical in values and order. Independent counting reproduced both word blocks for all 2,968 records with zero numerical error.

Both variants use overlapping frequencies: count / (L − k + 1), where L is sequence length and k is word length. A 200-base sequence has 199 two-letter windows or 198 three-letter windows. This retains the inspected iLearn normalization. The paper states 3-mers but writes a denominator of L; that discrepancy remains unresolved. The 3-mer alternative is not a verified copy of the paper’s exact features.

MCSE-Enhancer · normalization comparison · 15 October 2026

Does dividing by 198 or 200 change the result?

The two normalizations gave similar overall accuracy: 76.82% with iLearn and 76.48% with the paper’s literal denominator. The comparison did not establish a clear gain or loss. Some individual predictions changed, so the two implementations are not identical.

Exploratory repaired reconstruction. This tests one calculation in our repaired MCSE-Enhancer pipeline. It is not exact reproduction of the published results or independent confirmation. The task is identifying enhancers versus non-enhancers in the historical benchmark, not predicting enhancer strength or cell-specific experimental activity.

The calculation, in plain language

A 3-mer is a three-letter DNA word. A 200-letter sequence has 198 overlapping positions where such a word can start. The pinned iLearn implementation divides each word’s count by 198; a literal reading of Equation 7 in the paper divides it by the sequence length, 200. For a word counted 10 times, this means 10 / 198 ≈ 0.050505 versus 10 / 200 = 0.05.

Only the 64 standalone 3-mer frequencies changed. Each becomes 1% smaller, and their sum changes from 1.00 to 0.99. No DNA information is added or removed. The other 15,153 feature columns are exactly unchanged; both variants have 15,217 columns. This comparison does not change every normalization in the paper.

Paper denominator minus iLearn: −0.34 percentage points. The paired conditional 95% interval is −0.85 to +0.17 points. It includes zero: these results do not establish a clear advantage or disadvantage, and they do not prove equivalence. AUROC, a measure of how well scores rank the two classes, rose slightly from 0.8475 to 0.8481; that descriptive change does not establish a ranking improvement.

What this means

  • The overall difference is small. The paper-denominator version corrected 23 errors and introduced 33 new errors: 56 decisions changed, with 10 fewer correct predictions overall.
  • A small arithmetic change can affect a trained model. All five tree-based classifiers gave exactly the same saved probabilities. The neural-network component (MLP) changed, and those changed scores reached the combined model. A separate repeatability check supports numerical sensitivity as an explanation, but the precise cause has not been isolated.
  • This does not settle the original method. It compares two plausible interpretations in our implementation. It does not identify which calculation produced the authors’ published results, select a replacement model, or validate transfer to new experimental data.

The MLP used training-only standardization. There was no tuning or winner selection.

All 400 records in the contaminated historical test were excluded from fitting and scoring. No NEW_2026_DATA outcomes were used. MCSE-Enhancer · external evaluation plan · 15 October 2026

A new test with VISTA: the plan and preliminary screening

Independence is not yet verified. The protocol is defined, but the candidate set still needs broader overlap checks. No VISTA model training, predictions or performance evaluation have been performed. This remains a plan for evaluating a repaired reconstruction, not an exact reproduction of the published MCSE results.

The question is whether a model trained on historical enhancer annotations can rank different DNA fragments by experimentally measured activity. VISTA provides positive and negative reporter results for human DNA tested in mouse embryos. That is a new biological setting, so success here would show transfer to this particular assay—not establish performance in every cell or in lentiMPRA.

What the preliminary screen found

From the saved export’s 5,090 experiment rows, the protocol selects human reference allele 0, the hg38 genome assembly, embryonic stage e11.5, the hZR assay backbone and an explicit positive or negative curator call. The hZR assay uses an Hsp68 promoter and lacZ reporter with random transgenesis. Other species, variant alleles, stages and assay backbones are excluded.

1,847 candidates does not mean 1,847 independent examples. The final count and number of independent groups are still unknown. Positive and negative are the database’s experimental labels, not our predictions. A negative call means no reproducible positive result under this assay; it does not mean inactivity in every biological setting.

The 27 reference mismatches were excluded because the tested sequence did not match the supplied reference in either orientation. The 14 overlap exclusions are whole fragments containing an exact 200-base legacy sequence; one fragment can match several legacy records. Every possible 200-base starting position was checked against all 3,368 legacy development/test records, including reverse complements. Removing exact matches alone cannot rule out related sequences or shared genomic regions.

The model and the test stay separate

The primary model remains the original 2-mer repaired reconstruction (notebook_k2_full), chosen before the first MCSE benchmark. The later 3-mer and normalization results do not select its replacement. It would be trained only on the 2,968 legacy development records, with the fixed reported settings and repaired evaluation procedure. VISTA labels would not influence fitting, preprocessing, thresholds or model selection.

The historical test and the previously examined grouped folds cannot supply fresh confirmation. VISTA is the proposed next external test because it has experimentally curated positive and negative calls. Its label definition and fragment lengths differ from the old benchmark, so its future metrics must not be compared directly with our earlier benchmark accuracies.

What must happen before scoring

  1. Check genomic locations and similar sequences. Reconcile legacy hg19 and VISTA hg38 coordinates, exclude shared or nearby regions, and screen for broader similarity in both orientations.
  2. Group related fragments. Keep shared loci, overlapping fragments and related sequences together; remove entire linked groups that overlap the legacy exclusion set.
  3. Reserve the later lentiMPRA study. Screen against all 60,000 S10 design inserts and their verified locations. Exclude linked VISTA groups so the two studies can be assessed separately. This uses design information, not lentiMPRA activity outcomes.
  4. Freeze and verify the evaluation. Review label provenance, finalize the candidate groups and separate label file, and validate a saved full-training model. Seal predictions before joining them to the labels for reporting.
How longer fragments would be scored

The provisional fragments are 304–7,786 bases long, while the model takes 200 bases. The fixed rule uses 200-base windows every 50 bases in genomic-forward orientation, adds the last complete window, and uses the maximum score as one score per tested fragment. This would create 66,555 windows before the remaining exclusions. Windows are not independent experimental observations, and the whole-fragment label must not be treated as a measured label for each window.

The maximum is a prespecified transfer rule, not a calibrated probability of fragment activity. Longer fragments offer more chances for a high score. The plan therefore reports length and window-count distributions, length-only and GC-content score baselines, and results in fixed length groups: 200–499, 500–999, 1,000–1,999 and at least 2,000 bases. The mean of window scores is a secondary sensitivity check; the aggregation rule will not be selected using the results.

No final serialized MCSE model is currently available. The planned full-training stack needs 25 fits: six base models across three group-disjoint cross-fitting partitions, six full-training base fits and one combining-model fit on out-of-fold predictions. A validated save/load path and prediction replay check are required before external scoring.

Independence criteria and reporting rules

The exclusion universe includes all 2,968 development records and all 400 historical test records. Genomic screening requires verified coordinates, complete unique reciprocal assembly mapping, and exclusion of overlap or gaps of at most 1,000 bases. Near-sequence screening uses global edit distance of at most 40 between a legacy 200-base sequence and any contiguous candidate 200-base substring, in either orientation. This is an edit-distance rule, not a stated percent-identity threshold. Related candidate fragments form connected groups; accelerated searches must be validated against an exhaustive check.

The primary endpoint is fragment-level AUROC: how often a positive fragment receives a higher score than a negative one. Secondary summaries include average precision alongside positive prevalence, and MCC, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and ordinary accuracy at the unchanged 0.5 threshold. That threshold has not been calibrated for VISTA.

Uncertainty will use 2,000 whole-group bootstrap resamples with seed 20261015. At least 1,900 resamples must contain both classes for the planned interval; otherwise it is reported as not estimable. Before scoring, at least 100 distinct groups containing positives and 100 containing negatives are required, with mixed-label groups disclosed. This is a pragmatic minimum, not a power guarantee. Intervals are conditional on the fitted model and selected cohort, not all training or design uncertainty.

Public labels were accessible and label counts were inspected for feasibility; this is procedural separation, not blinded third-party validation. Original author parameter selection is not fully recoverable. Poor or inconclusive results will also be reported, and any model or protocol changes made after viewing outcomes become exploratory.
