Current research goal

Reproduce, evaluate, and connect

Reproduce published enhancer prediction methods, starting with iEnhancer-2L, then link their predictions to measured activity in HepG2, K562, and WTC11.

We begin with the papers and sequence files supplied by Prof. Dehzangi. Reconstructing a known method will establish what we can reproduce; the newer lentiMPRA study then lets us ask whether those predictions remain useful when activity is measured experimentally.

  1. Reproduce. Recover the original labels, rebuild iEnhancer-2L, and document agreement or differences from its published results.
  2. Evaluate fairly. Keep related sequences together and separate training, model selection, and final testing.
  3. Connect to new experiments. Compare the reproduced model’s scores with lentiMPRA activity in each cell, then consider retraining as a separate analysis.

Current stage: the iEnhancer-2L reconstruction and repaired MCSE-Enhancer comparisons are complete. The external VISTA plan and preliminary screening are now defined: 1,847 candidate fragments, 953 positive and 894 negative, after excluding 27 reference mismatches and 14 fragments containing exact legacy sequences. Independence is not yet verified. Genomic-location, similarity, family and lentiMPRA-design checks remain pending. There are no VISTA predictions or performance metrics; the original 2-mer primary model is retained. Exact MCSE reproduction and lentiMPRA specificity scores remain unresolved. 01 / Reproduction

Reproducing the original methods

Prof. Dehzangi supplied four original papers and the historical sequence files. They largely reuse one benchmark of 2,968 fragments, each 200 bases long. The labels come from chromatin-state annotations; the papers do not provide four independent activity experiments.

Why iEnhancer-2L first? Its equations, labeled sequences, and six DNA property tables are available, and its two SVMs are more tractable to reconstruct than the later ensembles. Its sequence features and both SVMs are implemented, with preprocessing, parameter choices, and grouped comparisons documented. The published evaluation remains a separate target. Reproducing its numerical accuracy is a goal, not a completed result.

Reconstruction is different from a new evaluation

Reconstruction rebuilds the method; numerical reproduction recovers its published result under specified settings. A cleaner grouped test is a new evaluation and must be reported separately. GC-only and ordinary 6-mer baselines have now been compared with the primary reconstruction, using the same grouped folds and training-only tuning. The component comparison examines structural summaries and rescaling. The scale-aware search examines tuning. The completed block-balance comparison tests equal training-distance contributions.

The old test cannot certify independence. All 100 strong test enhancers match training. Removing them leaves no strong test class. Define a new split that groups related sequences and overlapping loci, after verifying genome assembly. The review recovered all 742 strong, 742 weak, and 1,484 non-enhancer training labels by sequence matching.

Our iEnhancer-2L work is a reconstruction

We implemented the published equations and trained our own classifiers using the original labeled benchmark. The scripts in replication/ienhancer_2l/ are this project’s implementation. We have not located the authors’ complete training code, so this work is not a rerun of their original software.

Our grouped evaluation and baseline comparisons are complete. Exact reproduction of the paper’s leave-one-out results remains unresolved: counting conventions and the authors’ final SVM settings still need clarification, and our evaluation design differs. Original code, reference feature vectors, or a complete parameter record would help settle those differences. The reproduction guide records our implementation choices and results →

Why available code still needs checking

The MCSE notebooks were inspected at the saved author-repository version ↗ on 12 October 2026. The notebooks were inspected, not executed; these findings do not establish the exact code used to obtain the paper’s 2023 results.

02 / Experimental connection

Link the reproduced method to lentiMPRA

After reconstructing the original model, test whether its predictions relate to measured reporter activity in HepG2, K562, and WTC11.

The Agarwal, Inoue study (Nature, 2025) designed a joint library of 60,000 constructs. Each 230-base design includes a 200-base insert between two 15-base adapters. That shared insert length connects the historical sequence features to new measurements.

The working question

How well do features learned from historical enhancer annotations predict measured activity across cells, and where do they fail?

Why these cells and this study?

HepG2 is liver-tumor-derived, K562 is leukemia-derived, and WTC11 is an induced pluripotent stem-cell line. Testing the same DNA in these different settings supports a matched comparison. The choice follows experimental design and traceable measurements, not dataset size. The old benchmark’s embryonic H1ES cells are not WTC11.

First freeze the model; then consider adaptation

Freeze the model trained on historical annotations before comparing its scores with continuous activity in each cell. Retraining those features on lentiMPRA activity is a separate experiment. A single sequence-only score cannot predict different values for the same DNA in different cells without separate models or explicit cell inputs.

Strong/weak annotations are not high/low reporter measurements. Reproduce activity processing and define any active/inactive labels using appropriate controls. Exclude the two exact legacy-training matches in S10, check related loci, and keep the 149 reverse-complement pairs and all cell records of each sequence in the same split.

The 60,000 designs, 56,977 complete-case constructs, and 55,977 specificity-table members are distinct populations. Numerical specificity reproduction remains unresolved. Completing that step supports the transfer comparison; it does not establish a new biological finding.

What this comparison can tell us

It can describe shared or selective reporter activity among adequately measured fragments in these three cell lines. The library was selected partly using earlier activity measurements, so its activity proportions should not be generalized to all genomic DNA. Reporter activity is a measurement in the test system; it does not by itself identify which natural gene a sequence controls.

What is already checked

Membership recovered; numerical reproduction still open

The local audit recovered exactly 55,977 specificity-table names by requiring at least one replicate with ≥10 barcodes in each cell. Two control-name differences have a proposed explicit crosswalk, still awaiting original FASTA confirmation. Joint ENCODE and supplementary replicate measurements agree after separating no_BC; they are representations of the same measurements, not independent validation.

Use each experiment’s combined TSV or its three replicate TSVs. Combining both doubles the rows. The next task is to trace normalization, aggregation, filtering population, and rounding: direct score reconstruction still differs from the published values.

The local legacy “independent” test set contains 100 enhancer sequences already in development data. It cannot currently support sequence-independent evaluation. The downloader’s publication-receipt handling and the archived DNABERT launcher also remain unresolved.

Later / Assay comparison

How reliable is the activity evidence?

For regions with adequate K562 measurements in multiple assays, investigate whether activity calls remain stable under defensible coverage, orientation, and overlap criteria. A region absent from an active-call list may be untested or insufficiently measured.

A possible extension asks whether selective and broadly active constructs differ in the stability of their K562 evidence. Shared source measurements must be identified, and the number of independently measured overlapping loci must support the comparison.

Later / Motif analysis

Motif combinations and cell state

Synthetic MPRA experiments provide designed changes to motif identity and arrangement. The hematopoiesis collection offers a route to studying combinations that behave differently across cell states, with explicit sequence designs and controls.

This branch requires its own R-data import, definition of the activity scale, and matched comparison of synthetic constructs. It is a later mechanistic extension. Group related synthetic designs during evaluation; it does not replace the initial replication milestone.

From papers to analysis

How each study could contribute

The four original methods provide the replication targets. lentiMPRA provides the main experimental connection; the other seven newer works provide annotation, external comparisons, or later extensions. These are proposed uses; datasets must meet the requirements below before they can support our conclusions.

Annotate the measured DNA

ENCODE / SCREEN · Moore et al.

What we could do. Match measured regions to the same genome assembly, then add candidate regulatory classes and promoter proximity to our activity table. This could help describe whether shared and selective sequences have different genomic annotations.

What we must check. A candidate annotation is supporting context. Absence from the downloaded enhancer-like subset does not establish inactivity.

Possible reliability analysis

Assay comparison · Zhang et al.

What we could do. For sequences adequately measured by more than one K562 assay, compare agreement under different coverage, orientation, and processing choices. A candidate extension asks whether cell-selective sequences have more or less stable supporting evidence.

What we must check. This depends on enough comparable regions and independent measurements. Reused lentiMPRA data cannot count as independent validation; the original paper already studied assay disagreement.

Later motif analysis

Hematopoiesis · Frömel et al.

What we could do. Use the deliberately designed sequences to compare binding sites alone and in combinations. The study tested 64,400 synthetic sequences and offers a later way to test whether reproduced sequence features capture motif interactions.

What we must check. Analyze synthetic designs and mouse progenitor experiments in their own context, with matched controls and a defined activity scale. Reproducing a known interaction is a first milestone; a new contribution needs a question the paper has not answered.

Optional prediction evaluation

DNABERT-Enhancer · Sathian et al.

What we could do. After establishing the reproduced baseline, evaluate the available model on sequences that satisfy its input requirements and have suitable experimental evidence. Document where its enhancer predictions agree with measured activity and where they do not.

What we must check. The archived prediction launcher references a missing Python file and needs repair before inference. The local legacy test set is already known to overlap development data; define a clean evaluation set and check overlap with model training data before reporting performance. An annotation-prediction probability and a quantitative reporter measurement have different meanings; the task and metric must reflect that difference.

Optional interpretation tool

AlphaGenome · Avsec et al.

What we could do. For a focused set of regions or variants, examine predictions of relevant molecular readouts, such as gene expression or chromatin accessibility. These could suggest hypotheses to investigate after we understand the experimental measurements.

What we must check. Choose the genomic context, cell or tissue readout, and model version explicitly. Predictions are not independent experimental confirmation and do not directly reproduce a short-fragment reporter assay.

What we could do. A suitable Atlas release could let us retrieve existing predictions for selected variants instead of generating every prediction ourselves. Its potential role is hypothesis prioritization and interpretation.

What we must check. This is a preprint resource derived from predictions. We have the paper and supplements, not the full Atlas; data access, release version, score meaning, and applicability must be checked first.
