Our proposed scientific direction

The goal and the first milestone

Understand how enhancer activity depends on cellular context, and how reliable the supporting experimental evidence is.

Our working question is which DNA fragments show activity shared across measured cell settings, which favor one setting, and which patterns remain well supported after checking measurement quality. The first milestone is a documented activity table and a reproduction of a published comparison, with clear figures and limitations.

The specific original question is still being refined. A possible next question connects cell selectivity with the stability of K562 activity evidence across assays. We need enough comparable, independent measurements and a review of what the papers already answer before committing to that extension. A new prediction model is an option only if it helps address a defined question.

01 / Recommended first analysis

Why we recommend this starting analysis

We propose starting with a published experiment that measured the same DNA collection in HepG2, K562, and WTC11. Its matched design, measured activity, and available source tables fit our first question.

The lentiMPRA study by Agarwal, Inoue and colleagues (Nature, 2025) designed a joint library of 60,000 sequences for testing in all three cell lines. Here, a library means a collection of DNA pieces prepared for an experiment. Comparing the same fragments with the same overall method helps us investigate how activity changes with the cellular environment.

The working question

When the same DNA fragment is tested in three cellular settings, is its measured activity similar across them, or does it favor one setting?

Different biological settings, a shared experiment

HepG2 is a liver-tumor-derived cell line, K562 is a leukemia-derived cell line used to study blood-related biology, and WTC11 is a reprogrammed human stem-cell line. They offer different regulatory environments in which to test the same DNA. Our rationale is a useful, available comparison; these three models are not a comprehensive sample of human cells.

Why this paper rather than a larger dataset?

The recommendation is based on fit to the question, comparability, and a concrete result we can reproduce. More sequences help only if their measurements are usable. Frömel and colleagues tested 64,400 synthetic sequences and could be a better starting point for a question about interactions between DNA binding sites. The hematopoiesis branch is a real alternative; this is not a ranking of overall scientific quality or a claim that one dataset answers every question.

What we will do first

We will match the exact DNA constructs, check repeated measurements and barcode coverage, and reproduce a selected published comparison, beginning with Figure 5 and Supplementary Tables S10–S11. The 60,000 figure describes the designed library; the number of usable, matched sequences depends on quality checks.

The original study already examined cell specificity. Reproducing its result is our first goal, and any proposed extension needs its own assessment of feasibility and novelty. Differences can reflect both biology and experimental conditions, so they need careful interpretation.

What this comparison can tell us

It can describe shared or selective reporter activity among adequately measured fragments in these three cell lines. The library was selected partly using earlier activity measurements, so its activity proportions should not be generalized to all genomic DNA. Reporter activity is a measurement in the test system; it does not by itself identify which natural gene a sequence controls.

02 / A possible extension

How reliable is the activity evidence?

For regions with adequate K562 measurements in multiple assays, investigate whether activity calls remain stable under defensible coverage, orientation, and overlap criteria. A region absent from an active-call list may be untested or insufficiently measured.

A possible extension asks whether selective and broadly active constructs differ in the stability of their K562 evidence. Shared source measurements must be identified, and the number of independently measured overlapping loci must support the comparison.

03 / A mechanistic alternative

Motif combinations and cell state

Synthetic MPRA experiments provide designed changes to motif identity and arrangement. The hematopoiesis collection offers a route to studying combinations that behave differently across cell states, with explicit sequence designs and controls.

This branch requires its own R-data import, definition of the activity scale, and matched comparison of synthetic constructs. It is a possible starting analysis if understanding motif interactions becomes the priority, and an alternative if the cross-assay extension is not feasible.

From papers to analysis

How each study could contribute

The lentiMPRA comparison supplies the proposed baseline. The other seven works offer context, possible extensions, or alternative questions. These are proposed uses; datasets must meet the requirements below before they can support our conclusions.

Annotate the measured DNA

ENCODE / SCREEN · Moore et al.

What we could do. Match measured regions to the same genome assembly, then add candidate regulatory classes and promoter proximity to our activity table. This could help describe whether shared and selective sequences have different genomic annotations.

What we must check. A candidate annotation is supporting context. Absence from the downloaded enhancer-like subset does not establish inactivity.

Possible reliability analysis

Assay comparison · Zhang et al.

What we could do. For sequences adequately measured by more than one K562 assay, compare agreement under different coverage, orientation, and processing choices. A candidate extension asks whether cell-selective sequences have more or less stable supporting evidence.

What we must check. This depends on enough comparable regions and independent measurements. Reused lentiMPRA data cannot count as independent validation; the original paper already studied assay disagreement.

Alternative starting analysis

Hematopoiesis · Frömel et al.

What we could do. Use the deliberately designed sequences to compare binding sites alone and in combinations. The study tested 64,400 synthetic sequences and offers a strong alternative starting point if our main question becomes how DNA features interact.

What we must check. Analyze synthetic designs and mouse progenitor experiments in their own context, with matched controls and a defined activity scale. Reproducing a known interaction is a first milestone; a new contribution needs a question the paper has not answered.

Possible developmental examples

VISTA · Kosicki et al.

What we could do. Where sequence identity and experimental metadata permit, build examples that place cultured-cell reporter activity alongside developmental tissue activity. VISTA could also support a separate project on developmental specificity or sequence variants.

What we must check. Human source DNA may have been tested in mouse embryos. Keep tissue, developmental stage, sequence, and reporter design explicit; this is a different biological setting from our three cell lines.

Optional prediction evaluation

DNABERT-Enhancer · Sathian et al.

What we could do. If prediction becomes part of the question, evaluate the available model on sequences that satisfy its input requirements and have suitable experimental evidence. Document where its enhancer predictions agree with measured activity and where they do not.

What we must check. Audit overlap with training data before evaluating performance. An annotation-prediction probability and a quantitative reporter measurement have different meanings; the task and metric must reflect that difference.

Read the paper ↗ Data or tool ↗ Our collection →

Optional interpretation tool

AlphaGenome · Avsec et al.

What we could do. For a focused set of regions or variants, examine predictions of relevant molecular readouts, such as gene expression or chromatin accessibility. These could suggest hypotheses to investigate after we understand the experimental measurements.

What we must check. Choose the genomic context, cell or tissue readout, and model version explicitly. Predictions are not independent experimental confirmation and do not directly reproduce a short-fragment reporter assay.

Read the paper ↗ Data or tool ↗ Our collection →

Optional precomputed predictions

AlphaGenome Atlas · preprint

What we could do. A suitable Atlas release could let us retrieve existing predictions for selected variants instead of generating every prediction ourselves. Its potential role is hypothesis prioritization and interpretation.

What we must check. This is a preprint resource derived from predictions. We have the paper and supplements, not the full Atlas; data access, release version, score meaning, and applicability must be checked first.
