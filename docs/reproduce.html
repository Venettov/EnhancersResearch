Reconstructing published methods · iEnhancer-2L and MCSE-Enhancer

From the original files to a reproducible analysis

This guide documents our reconstruction of the two classifiers in iEnhancer-2L, using the original data and a local Python environment. VS Code provides an optional workspace for editing scripts and running commands; Python performs the calculations. The numbered steps cover iEnhancer-2L. A separate MCSE-Enhancer section documents the repaired reconstruction, completed benchmark and remaining differences from the author method.

Completed · updated 15 October 2026: both SVMs, grouped evaluation, baselines, feature sensitivity, structural-component comparisons, the scale-aware search, training-only block balancing, and the repaired MCSE-Enhancer benchmark, including its word-length and normalization comparisons. The external VISTA plan and preliminary screening are documented; independence is not yet verified.
Next: complete the independence checks and validate the final model before producing external predictions. The historical published performance has not been reproduced. External plan and preliminary screening → · Reconstruction workspace →

Our iEnhancer-2L implementation is a reconstruction. We wrote the feature-extraction and training code from the published method and used the original labeled data. The authors’ complete iEnhancer-2L training code has not been located in the reviewed materials. Our grouped results do not establish exact reproduction of the paper’s leave-one-out results. Step 05 / Evaluation design

Decide what each result will mean before training

A fold is a portion of the data held aside while a model learns from the rest. In nested validation, inner folds select settings and outer folds estimate performance on examples unused by that selection.

Track A: historical reconstruction. Use S1's 2,968 examples for enhancer identification and its 1,484 enhancers for strength classification. The paper selected feature settings by 5-fold cross-validation, then reported jackknife (leave-one-out) results. A fixed-setting leave-one-out pass needs 2,968 + 1,484 fits; tuning adds more. A quick 5-fold trial is a debugging step, not a reproduced jackknife result.

Track B: a new grouped evaluation. All 2,968 sequence intervals were checked against hg19, using zero-based, end-exclusive coordinates. Records are connected if their intervals overlap or are at most 1,000 bases apart on the same chromosome, or if their 200-base sequences differ by at most 40 global edits in either orientation. Connected groups stay together in 5 outer folds and 3 inner folds, with seed 42 and balancing across the three classes. These are project choices, not the paper’s leave-one-out procedure or CD-HIT identity rule. They reduce specified leakage risks without proving complete independence. The policy, assignments, and checks are saved before training. Any scaler or tuning procedure must use only the relevant training folds.

The old 400-record test is unsuitable as independent evidence. Its 100 strong enhancers are already in training. Removing them leaves no strong class for strength evaluation. Exact matching alone also does not prove independence from related sequences. See the documented overlap findings.

Published feature choices were themselves selected using this benchmark. Holding those choices fixed is appropriate for reconstructing the published configuration, but it is not a fresh, fully independent estimate of the complete selection procedure. Report the two tracks separately. Cross-validation and grouped evaluation reference.

Step 06 / Implementation specification

Turn each sequence into 4,105 numbers

The feature extractor is implemented and tested against the equations and supplement, with unresolved author-code choices documented. PseKNC combines DNA word frequencies with sequence-derived structural descriptors. They are not measured chromatin or DNA-looping data.

Recorded interpretation: sample standard deviation reproduces S2’s normalized entries within their two-decimal rounding (maximum difference 0.004724). The primary reconstruction uses overlapping 6-letter windows and position-based dinucleotide pairs. Four additional variants preserve alternative counting, rounded-table and population-normalization choices; none has been selected by model accuracy.

python -m unittest discover -s replication/ienhancer_2l -p "test_*.py" -v
python replication/ienhancer_2l/build_features.py --prepared replication/ienhancer_2l/output --receipts replication/ienhancer_2l/checkpoints/2026-10-13-groups-features/reference_receipts.json --output replication/ienhancer_2l/output/groups-features

The command uses saved reference receipts for an offline rerun. It writes fold assignments, audits, and five compressed matrix variants: 2,968 × 4,105 for layer I and 1,484 × 4,105 for layer II. Exact conventions, validation, output fields and fresh-reference commands →. No classifier is trained by this command.

  1. Transcribe the six columns in S2: Rise, Roll, Shift, Slide, Tilt, Twist, for all 16 dinucleotides. Preserve their labels, order, and source. Verify normalization across the 16 values per property using equation 9. Compare against S2's rounded normalized table and record the standard-deviation convention and precision.
  2. Count the 4,096 possible 6-letter DNA words in a fixed order and normalize the counts. Record the counting stride and treatment of end positions.
  3. For each distance j = 1…9, compute the average squared difference between the six normalized properties of dinucleotide pairs, following equations 7–8.
  4. Combine those nine correlation terms with the 4,096 frequencies using equation 6. Use w = 0.1 for layer I and w = 0.4 for layer II. Both use k = 6 and λ = 9 (equation 13).
The calculation, in compact notation
C(i, i+j) = mean over 6 properties of (P(D_i) - P(D_(i+j)))²
theta[j] = sum of C(i, i+j), i=1…L-j-1, divided by (L-j-1)
Z = sum(frequencies) + w * sum(theta)
features = [frequency / Z, …, w * theta[j] / Z, …]

L is sequence length; D_i is the dinucleotide indexed at position i; P is a normalized property value. The notation follows equations 6–8, subject to the indexing check below.

Resolve before calling this exact reproduction: the text near equation 6 and the Figure 2 caption say “non-overlapping,” while the L − j − 1 denominator uses position-based indexing. Recover an author implementation or reference feature vectors to settle the intended stride/indexing. Also settle use of rounded S2 values versus recomputed normalization and any subsequent feature scaling. Record an explicit interpretation if the original cannot be recovered; do not select one simply because it gives a higher score.

Acceptance checks: each vector has 4,105 finite, nonnegative entries and sums approximately to 1 under equation 6. Test a constant-base sequence (zero structural differences), a hand-calculated short example, and both weights. Check that input order never changes label alignment. Structural-property normalization is across the fixed property table; a separate model scaler, if used, must be fitted only on training data.

Step 07 / Model implementation

Train two separate SVM classifiers

  1. Layer I: use all 2,968 rows. Positive = enhancer (strong or weak); negative = non-enhancer. Build features with w = 0.1.
  2. Layer II: use only the 1,484 known enhancers. Positive = strong; negative = weak. Build features with w = 0.4.
  3. Use an RBF SVM and explicitly record C, gamma, scaling, class weights, solver tolerance, shrinking, software version, and score orientation. The paper describes grid search but does not provide the final C/gamma values in these materials. Scikit-learn's gamma="scale" is not a recovered paper setting.
  4. Search candidate settings using training-only validation. For a reconstruction attempt, record a predeclared logarithmic C/gamma search and every choice; do not tune against final test labels or repeatedly chase Table 1. A stricter nested evaluation must keep all selection inside each outer training fold.
  5. For each held-out example, save its ID, true label, predicted label, continuous decision score, and fold. For a new query, run layer II only if layer I predicts enhancer. Evaluate the paper's layer-II task on known enhancers separately from this end-to-end cascade.

The RBF kernel measures similarity between feature vectors. C controls the penalty for training errors; gamma controls how locally that similarity acts. Their values change predictions and must be recorded.

Start small: validate dimensions, labels, and score signs on a small debugging subset. Do not publish that subset's performance. Then run the full predefined protocol. The completed implementation uses the primary feature version without additional scaling. The parameter grid and selection rules were committed before fitting; all inner searches exclude the relevant outer-test groups.

Recorded protocol: 48 combinations of C and gamma, selected by mean inner-fold balanced accuracy; exact ties favor smaller C, then gamma. After the five outer evaluations, a separate five-fold search selects each full-data final model. Its selection score is not a new independent test result. Complete frozen protocol →

Baseline comparisons · explained 14 October 2026

What the DNA-word comparison tells us

Counting short DNA words was competitive with the reconstructed iEnhancer-2L features. For distinguishing strong from weak enhancers, ordinary 6-mers had the higher observed accuracy. That is a useful lead for the next experiment, rather than an explanation of the biology.

The main result: ordinary 6-mers reached 63.68% mean accuracy for strong/weak classification, compared with 60.98% for our primary reconstruction: a gain of 2.69 percentage points, calculated before rounding. Roughly, that is 64 correct classifications per 100 examples instead of 61. These labels describe the original benchmark, not measured reporter activity in the new study.

What each model reads

A 6-mer is a six-letter DNA word. Sliding a six-letter window along ACGTACGT produces ACGTAC, CGTACG, and GTACGT. Each 200-base sequence contains 195 such overlapping windows. The ordinary representation records the frequencies of all 4,096 possible words.

PseKNC, the paper’s feature representation, combines those word frequencies with nine summaries derived from DNA structural-property tables. It also rescales both parts using a factor that depends on the sequence. Both approaches use the same type of SVM classifier. The simpler representation has 4,096 features instead of 4,105; it is not a radically smaller model.

Possible reasons for the higher strength accuracy

  • Word frequencies may already capture useful patterns. Their observed performance shows an association with the benchmark labels. It does not identify which words matter or establish that a particular word regulates a gene.
  • The extra information may not help this particular task. The structural summaries and their weighting could change how useful word patterns influence the classifier. More descriptors do not automatically provide more useful information.
  • Two things changed together. Ordinary 6-mers remove both the nine structural summaries and the PseKNC rescaling factor. This comparison cannot tell which change contributed to the gain. Feature scale, the finite parameter search, and variation in this dataset also remain possible contributors.

What is established: a modest gain on these held-out benchmark records. What remains unknown: its cause and whether it will persist on independent experimental data. The original model is retained; no replacement was selected from these results.

How certain is the difference?

The paired conditional 95% interval for the 6-mer strength-accuracy gain is +0.10 to +5.15 percentage points. It comes from 1,000 resamples of whole sequence groups within each fold, comparing both methods on the same resampled records. Models and folds stay fixed: this does not include uncertainty from retraining or choosing the analysis.

The comparison is exploratory, with several alternatives examined and no adjustment for multiple comparisons. The plan was recorded and hashed before these comparison fits, after the primary results were already known; it was not externally preregistered. The slightly positive lower bound is not confirmation of a general advantage.

ROC AUC, which measures how well scores rank the classes, was 0.688 for ordinary 6-mers versus 0.668 for the primary strength classifier. The interval for that difference spans zero (−0.0023 to +0.0427). GC-only accuracy was also close to primary, although its strength AUC was lower, at 0.645. Similar accuracy does not mean the models make the same predictions or rank sequences equally well.

Counting details also matter

Using separate, non-overlapping six-letter blocks gives only 33 windows per sequence instead of 195. With parameter retuning, this version reached 73.08% identification accuracy and 59.57% strength accuracy. Alternative repeated-word counting and property-table normalization produced smaller aggregate changes, but sometimes changed individual predictions. None of these comparisons identifies which implementation the original authors used.

Component comparison · completed 14 October 2026

Separating structural summaries from rescaling

Ordinary DNA-word frequencies still had the highest observed accuracy for strong versus weak enhancers in the retuned comparison. Testing the two extra components separately shows that both affect the result, and that their effects depend on the model settings.

In simple terms: adding more descriptions of a sequence changes how the model judges similarity. Rescaling changes the size of those numbers. Neither change automatically improves prediction; the features and the SVM settings need to work together.

Four versions of the same comparison

The words are the frequencies of 4,096 possible six-letter DNA patterns. The structural summaries are nine numbers calculated from a fixed table of DNA properties; they are not direct measurements of three-dimensional DNA shape. Rescaling divides the numbers by a factor calculated separately for each sequence.

What the results mean

  • Word counts remain a useful baseline. With retuning, rescaling words alone reduced strong/weak accuracy by 2.50 percentage points; adding structural summaries without rescaling reduced it by 3.23 points.
  • The effects interact. Combining both changes gives 60.98%, so the two decreases cannot simply be added. Adding summaries to already rescaled words changed accuracy by only −0.20 points, with considerable uncertainty.
  • Model settings are part of the explanation. When all versions shared the primary model’s settings, rescaling words increased accuracy slightly instead of decreasing it. The direction therefore depends on the tuning procedure.

Conclusion at this stage: the comparison identifies sensitivity to feature scale and model settings. It does not establish that structural information is biologically unhelpful or that ordinary words will perform better on independent experimental data. The original reference model is preserved.

What happened with shared SVM settings?

Each outer fold used the same primary-model settings for all four versions. These settings were selected for full PseKNC, so this is a sensitivity check rather than a comparison of individually optimized models.

Uncertainty, formulas, and a numerical clue

For strength classification with retuning, the paired conditional 95% intervals are −4.62 to −0.50 points for rescaling words alone, −5.68 to −0.78 for adding unscaled summaries, and −2.80 to +2.40 for adding summaries after rescaling. All planned accuracy-contrast intervals for the retuned enhancer-identification models include zero.

Intervals use 1,000 paired whole-group resamples. They keep the fitted models and folds fixed, exclude retraining and analysis-selection uncertainty, and have no multiple-comparison correction. This follow-up was specified after seeing the baseline results on the same benchmark; it remains exploratory.

Let F be word frequencies, T = w × theta the weighted summaries, and Z = sum(F) + w × sum(theta). The four versions are F, F/Z, [F,T], and [F,T]/Z, retaining weights 0.1 for identification and 0.4 for strength. Both rescaled versions use the same sequence-specific Z. Rescaled words still carry structural information through Z.

For strength classification, the nine structural coordinates contribute 93.5% of total squared feature distance across 20,000 sampled sequence pairs before rescaling, and 77.2% afterward. The SVM uses these distances to judge similarity. This shows how a small block of features can strongly influence the model; it does not prove the sole cause of the accuracy difference.

What the results mean

  • The simple baseline remains useful. Ordinary words lead full PseKNC by 3.44 percentage points on strength accuracy. This is evidence within this comparison, not a confirmed advantage on new experiments.
  • Structural summaries have caught up substantially when used without rescaling. Their accuracy rises from 60.45% to 62.13%. The gap behind ordinary words shrinks from 3.23 to 0.67 points, and the uncertainty interval includes either method doing better.

What changed in the explanation: the earlier result cannot be summarized as “extra structural information hurts.” Model settings affect how that information is used. The new search adjusts the overall distance scale; it does not balance the influence of the word and structural feature blocks.

Why did the percentages change?

The same sequences, labels, feature definitions, and evaluation groups were retained. Different SVM settings were tested and selected using training data only. Every feature version received 90 combinations instead of the previous 48. The new candidates are different, not simply the old candidates plus extra ones; a changed search need not improve held-out results. Ordinary-word strength accuracy changes from 63.68% to 62.80%, while full PseKNC changes from 60.98% to 59.37%. These changes reflect the search procedure as a whole, not scale adjustment alone.

How the scale-aware search works

Within each inner training set, m is the median squared Euclidean distance across all unordered pairs of distinct training records. The diagonal is excluded; zero distances between distinct records are retained. The kernel is exp(−alpha × distance² / m), equivalent to gamma = alpha / m. Validation and test records never enter this scale calculation. The outer refit recomputes m on its outer training records only.

The original six C values are retained. The 15 alpha values are powers of 2 with exponents −19, −17, …, 9, giving 90 settings for each of the three inner folds. Selection uses mean balanced accuracy; exact ties favor smaller C, then smaller alpha. A uniform change of feature units cancels in the kernel, but the relative influence of individual features and blocks remains unchanged.

No selected alpha reaches a search endpoint. Words plus summaries reach maximum C in three strength folds and one identification fold; full PseKNC reaches it in two identification folds. Finite search coverage remains a limitation. The grid was not expanded after seeing the results, and no new full-data model or replacement model was selected.

What are 2-mers and 3-mers?

The 27 reference mismatches were excluded because the tested sequence did not match the supplied reference in either orientation. The 14 overlap exclusions are whole fragments containing an exact 200-base legacy sequence; one fragment can match several legacy records. Every possible 200-base starting position was checked against all 3,368 legacy development/test records, including reverse complements. Removing exact matches alone cannot rule out related sequences or shared genomic regions.

The model and the test stay separate

The primary model remains the original 2-mer repaired reconstruction (notebook_k2_full), chosen before the first MCSE benchmark. The later 3-mer and normalization results do not select its replacement. It would be trained only on the 2,968 legacy development records, with the fixed reported settings and repaired evaluation procedure. VISTA labels would not influence fitting, preprocessing, thresholds or model selection.

The historical test and the previously examined grouped folds cannot supply fresh confirmation. VISTA is the proposed next external test because it has experimentally curated positive and negative calls. Its label definition and fragment lengths differ from the old benchmark, so its future metrics must not be compared directly with our earlier benchmark accuracies.

What must happen before scoring

  1. Check genomic locations and similar sequences. Reconcile legacy hg19 and VISTA hg38 coordinates, exclude shared or nearby regions, and screen for broader similarity in both orientations.
  2. Group related fragments. Keep shared loci, overlapping fragments and related sequences together; remove entire linked groups that overlap the legacy exclusion set.
  3. Reserve the later lentiMPRA study. Screen against all 60,000 S10 design inserts and their verified locations. Exclude linked VISTA groups so the two studies can be assessed separately. This uses design information, not lentiMPRA activity outcomes.
  4. Freeze and verify the evaluation. Review label provenance, finalize the candidate groups and separate label file, and validate a saved full-training model. Seal predictions before joining them to the labels for reporting.
How longer fragments would be scored

The provisional fragments are 304–7,786 bases long, while the model takes 200 bases. The fixed rule uses 200-base windows every 50 bases in genomic-forward orientation, adds the last complete window, and uses the maximum score as one score per tested fragment. This would create 66,555 windows before the remaining exclusions. Windows are not independent experimental observations, and the whole-fragment label must not be treated as a measured label for each window.

The maximum is a prespecified transfer rule, not a calibrated probability of fragment activity. Longer fragments offer more chances for a high score. The plan therefore reports length and window-count distributions, length-only and GC-content score baselines, and results in fixed length groups: 200–499, 500–999, 1,000–1,999 and at least 2,000 bases. The mean of window scores is a secondary sensitivity check; the aggregation rule will not be selected using the results.

No final serialized MCSE model is currently available. The planned full-training stack needs 25 fits: six base models across three group-disjoint cross-fitting partitions, six full-training base fits and one combining-model fit on out-of-fold predictions. A validated save/load path and prediction replay check are required before external scoring.

Independence criteria and reporting rules

The exclusion universe includes all 2,968 development records and all 400 historical test records. Genomic screening requires verified coordinates, complete unique reciprocal assembly mapping, and exclusion of overlap or gaps of at most 1,000 bases. Near-sequence screening uses global edit distance of at most 40 between a legacy 200-base sequence and any contiguous candidate 200-base substring, in either orientation. This is an edit-distance rule, not a stated percent-identity threshold. Related candidate fragments form connected groups; accelerated searches must be validated against an exhaustive check.

The primary endpoint is fragment-level AUROC: how often a positive fragment receives a higher score than a negative one. Secondary summaries include average precision alongside positive prevalence, and MCC, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and ordinary accuracy at the unchanged 0.5 threshold. That threshold has not been calibrated for VISTA.

Uncertainty will use 2,000 whole-group bootstrap resamples with seed 20261015. At least 1,900 resamples must contain both classes for the planned interval; otherwise it is reported as not estimable. Before scoring, at least 100 distinct groups containing positives and 100 containing negatives are required, with mixed-label groups disclosed. This is a pragmatic minimum, not a power guarantee. Intervals are conditional on the fitted model and selected cohort, not all training or design uncertainty.

Public labels were accessible and label counts were inspected for feasibility; this is procedural separation, not blinded third-party validation. Original author parameter selection is not fully recoverable. Poor or inconclusive results will also be reported, and any model or protocol changes made after viewing outcomes become exploratory.

Screening evidence and downloads

The recorded audit checked 26 pinned input hashes. A separate verification matched two official VISTA checksums and independently confirmed the same 14 exact-overlap exclusions. Ten focused software tests passed. These checks support the preliminary screening; they do not certify independence. No new model was fitted or scored, and no lentiMPRA activity outcomes were read.

Step 09 / Later experimental connection

Then connect the reconstructed model to lentiMPRA

After completing the planned external evaluation and its independence checks, use Agarwal, Inoue and colleagues (Nature, 2025) for the later cell-by-cell activity comparison. The required collection is NEW_2026_DATA/02_LENTIMPRA_ENCODE_2025, rather than every new resource.

Before reporting a result

A clear stopping point for each stage

  • Data ready: the preparation script passes and source labels/hashes are saved.
  • Method ready: counting, normalization, and SVM decisions are documented; feature tests pass.
  • Evaluation ready: folds and tuning are fixed, held-out predictions are saved, and contaminated historical results are labeled.
  • Experimental transfer ready: model frozen, experimental processing checked, and independent transfer examples defined.

The recorded work covers data preparation, grouped folds, feature reconstruction, both trained SVMs, their first nested evaluation, and baseline, feature-sensitivity, component, scale-aware, and training-only block-balance comparisons. The repaired MCSE-Enhancer benchmark and its word-length and normalization comparisons are also complete. The external VISTA plan and preliminary screening are complete, but independence is not yet verified and there are no external predictions or metrics. Review the remaining checks before evaluation.
