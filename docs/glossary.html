EnhancersResearch / Glossary

Glossary

Plain-language explanations of the cells, DNA, experiments, and analysis behind this research.

Plain-language definitions explain the biology, software, and evaluation behind this research.

We are reproducing an earlier enhancer prediction method, then testing how its predictions relate to activity measured in different cells. This glossary explains the biology, model inputs, and fair evaluation needed for that work. Each entry includes a meaning, a connection to this project, and places to read more.

Source material may use technical language. The explanations here are written for this project; linked papers and reference sites retain their original language.

AUPRC

A summary of the trade-off between finding positives and avoiding false positive calls. It is especially useful when positives are uncommon, and its baseline depends on their frequency.

Why it matters here

Scores from test sets with different class balances may not be directly comparable.

Reading the evidence

Balanced accuracy

The average of the correctly detected proportion within each true class. Each class has equal weight, even when one class has many more examples.

Why it matters here

We use it to choose model settings within training folds. It is different from ordinary accuracy, which counts the proportion correct across all examples together.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · balanced accuracy

Baseline

A reference method used to judge whether a more complicated method adds useful predictive information. A baseline can be simple without being weak.

Why it matters here

Our GC-only and ordinary six-letter-word models provide comparisons for full PseKNC. We evaluate them using the same grouped splits, while retaining the stated tuning and uncertainty limits.

Benchmark

A specified dataset and evaluation procedure used to compare methods. Its labels describe a particular task, not every possible biological function.

Why it matters here

Our historical benchmark has 2,968 training sequences. Its 400-record test set overlaps training and cannot certify sequence-independent performance.

Bootstrap resampling

Repeatedly drawing samples with replacement from the observed data and recalculating a result to explore its variability. With replacement means an example may be drawn more than once.

Why it matters here

Our paired comparisons resample whole sequence groups together while retaining fitted models and folds. These intervals omit uncertainty from retraining and from choosing analyses after earlier results.

Reading the evidence

Classification sensitivity / recall

The proportion of actual positive examples correctly identified as positive. It asks how many of the positives the classifier finds.

Why it matters here

For identification, positives are enhancers; for the strength task, positives are strong enhancers. This metric differs from a feature-sensitivity experiment, which asks how predictions change when calculations change.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · prediction metrics

Reading the evidence

Classification specificity

The proportion of actual negative examples correctly identified as negative. It asks how well the classifier avoids positive calls for negative examples.

Why it matters here

Negatives are non-enhancers in layer I and weak enhancers in layer II. This classification metric is different from cell specificity, which describes how activity varies across cell types.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · prediction metrics

Reading the evidence

Confidence interval

A range calculated to express uncertainty under a specified statistical procedure. Its meaning depends on the data, assumptions, and sources of variability included in that procedure.

Why it matters here

Our conditional intervals keep models and folds fixed. They do not capture every source of uncertainty, and an interval spanning zero does not prove that two methods are equivalent.

Sources & further reading

  • SciPy · bootstrap resampling and confidence intervals

Confounding / selection bias

A comparison can be misleading if another difference influences the result, or if the chosen samples do not represent the population we want to discuss.

Why it matters here

The matched library was partly selected using earlier measurements. Its activity proportions should not be generalized to all genomic DNA.

Construct

A deliberately assembled piece of DNA used in an experiment. It can contain the sequence being tested, a promoter, a reporter, and an identifying barcode. The surrounding support DNA is often called the backbone.

Why it matters here

Coverage

Why it matters here

Low coverage can make a result uncertain. Missing evidence is not the same thing as measured inactivity.

Reading the evidence

Cross-validation / fold

Testing a model by dividing data into parts called folds. Each turn reserves one part and trains on the others, so examples can be assessed without being used to fit that turn’s model.

Why it matters here

Our evaluation keeps related sequences in the same fold. Five outer folds provide held-out predictions; the inner folds are used separately to choose settings.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · cross-validation and grouped splits

Reading the evidence

Data leakage

When information from data meant to test a model influences its training or selection. The model can then appear more accurate than it would be on genuinely new examples.

Why it matters here

All 100 strong examples in the inspected historical test set also occur in training. Independent evaluation requires separating related sequences and keeping test labels out of model selection.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · data leakage

Decision score

A model’s numerical output used to place an example on one side of its decision boundary. It is not automatically a probability or a measurement of biological activity.

Why it matters here

Our saved models use zero as the boundary: positive means enhancer in layer I and strong in layer II. Magnitudes should not be compared as a common biological scale across different models.

Enhancer

A DNA region that can increase the use of a gene in an appropriate cellular setting. It acts like a context-dependent volume control: it helps determine how much a gene is used, sometimes from far away along the DNA.

Why it matters here

FASTA

A plain-text format for DNA or protein sequences. Each record starts with a name line beginning with >, followed by the sequence letters.

Why it matters here

Feature scaling / block scaling

Changing the numerical scale of model inputs. When features are compared by distance, large numerical values can have more influence even if they carry no more useful information.

Why it matters here

Our follow-ups test rescaling and balancing the word and structural blocks. Learned scaling values use training data only. Equal distance contribution does not mean equal biological importance or guaranteed improvement.

A feature vector is a list of numbers describing one example. A feature matrix stacks those lists into a table: examples are rows and features are columns.

Why it matters here

Our primary representation assigns 4,105 numbers to each DNA sequence: 4,096 word frequencies and nine structural summaries, combined using the recorded PseKNC formula. The SVM receives these numbers rather than the raw DNA letters.

Genetic variant

A difference in DNA sequence compared with another sequence. A difference can affect one letter or involve inserted or deleted letters. Many variants have little or no effect in a given setting.

Why it matters here

Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC)

A classification score that uses all four outcomes: correct positives, correct negatives, false positives, and missed positives. Its usual range is −1 to 1; 1 means perfect agreement.

Why it matters here

It summarizes a particular prediction task. It is not a measure of how strongly an enhancer is active.

A group of proteins that helps DNA-bound activators communicate with the machinery that starts copying a gene into RNA. It is one of several participants in enhancer–promoter communication.

A motif is a short recurring DNA pattern that may be recognized by a regulatory protein. “Regulatory grammar” asks how such patterns work together: their order, spacing, and combinations.

Why it matters here

Reading the evidence

Positive / negative class

The two labels in a binary prediction task. Positive is the category the test is trying to detect; negative is the other category. A false positive assigns positive to a truly negative example, and a false negative misses a truly positive one.

Why it matters here

Positive means enhancer in layer I, but strong enhancer in layer II. Negative means non-enhancer or weak, respectively. The words do not mean good and bad, and depend on the reference labels for the task.

DNA & gene regulation

Promoter

Computational models

RapidFuzz / edit distance

RapidFuzz is software for comparing text strings. Our use counts the minimum letter insertions, deletions, and substitutions needed to turn one DNA sequence into another: its edit distance.

Why it matters here

We compare both DNA orientations and use these distances, together with genomic proximity, to group related sequences before evaluation. A small edit distance alone does not establish shared biological function.

  • RapidFuzz · Levenshtein edit distance

Computational models

RBF SVM / RBF kernel

A support vector machine (SVM) with a similarity rule called a radial basis function (RBF). Examples with nearby numerical features count as more similar. This lets the classifier learn a curved boundary between groups.

Why it matters here

Our two classifiers use this rule to compare DNA feature vectors. Gamma controls how quickly similarity falls with distance; C controls the penalty for training errors. Feature scaling and these settings can change predictions.

Sources & further reading

Replicate

A repeated measurement used to assess consistency. Biological replicates repeat the biological experiment; technical replicates repeat measurement or processing of the same biological material.

Why it matters here

Counting the same measurements again in a combined file does not create new independent evidence.

Reporter activity

A reporter is a measurable output built into an experimental system, such as RNA or a visible signal. Its activity helps researchers detect how a tested DNA sequence affects that system.

Why it matters here

It measures behavior in the test. It does not by itself prove that the sequence controls a particular gene at its natural location.

RNA polymerase II

An enzyme, made of several protein parts, that copies DNA into RNA. It produces the initial RNA copies of protein-coding genes and many noncoding RNA genes.

RNA output helps us measure reporter activity. More reporter RNA does not by itself identify a natural target gene or demonstrate a particular enhancer–promoter contact.

Reading the evidence

ROC AUC / AUROC

A measure of how well model scores rank positive examples above negative ones across decision cutoffs. A value of 0.5 means chance-level ranking; 1.0 means perfect ranking. It is not the percentage classified correctly. The name means area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.

Why it matters here

Here, positive means enhancer in layer I and strong enhancer in layer II. ROC AUC 0.841 does not mean 84.1% accuracy. We average rankings within each held-out fold; scores from different fitted models are not ranked together.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · ROC AUC from prediction scores
  • scikit-learn · prediction metrics

Tau (τ)

A score sometimes used to summarize how concentrated activity is across contexts. Its meaning depends on the exact formula and on how the input measurements were transformed.

Why it matters here

The report discusses a possible specificity measure. Reproducing the source study’s numerical scores remains unresolved; a familiar formula is not enough to assume equivalence.

Reading the evidence

Threshold / decision boundary

A cutoff used to turn a numerical result into a decision, such as assigning one class rather than another. Changing the cutoff can change labels without changing the underlying scores.

Why it matters here

Our SVM prediction rule uses zero: above zero means enhancer in layer I and strong in layer II. The eight server disagreements are near these boundaries. We did not adjust the cutoffs to make the models agree.

Sources & further reading

  • scikit-learn · decision thresholds

Transcription factor

A protein that helps control whether and how strongly DNA is copied into RNA. Many recognize particular DNA patterns, but their effects also depend on other molecules around them.

WTC11

A human induced pluripotent stem-cell line. Adult skin cells were reprogrammed into a state from which they can develop into many cell types under suitable conditions. These are not embryonic stem cells.

Agarwal, Inoue et al. (Nature, 2025) tested the same DNA in WTC11, HepG2, and K562. This is why WTC11 is part of our planned comparison: it supplies measurements in a stem-cell setting alongside the two tumor-derived lines.
