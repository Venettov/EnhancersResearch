Four original papers, connected to eight newer works.

The original reading materials supplied by Prof. Dehzangi are listed first. Their sequence methods largely reuse one historical benchmark. The newer collection contains eight distinct works represented by ten publication files, with preprints grouped alongside their published versions. Together, this page covers 12 distinct works.

The original PDFs are tracked in the repository. Each original paper links to its corresponding data; the shared benchmark and iEnhancer-2L supplements are not separate experimental validations.

2016L01

Liu et al.

iEnhancer-2L: a two-layer predictor for identifying enhancers and their strength by pseudo k-tuple nucleotide composition

Bioinformatics

Two SVM classifiers use DNA sequence counts and structural descriptors to identify enhancers and classify strong versus weak annotations. Its 2,968-sequence benchmark is the foundation reused by the other original methods.

  • Supplied article PDF ↗
  • Corresponding benchmark and supplements →
  • Supplement S1 · labeled sequences ↗
  • Supplement S2 · property tables ↗
Original materials · Prof. Dehzangi

Both SVMs are trained with a frozen grouped evaluation; mean accuracies are 76.21% and 60.98%. Exact historical performance remains unreproduced.

Read the source ↗
2016L02

Jia & He

EnhancerPred: a predictor for discovering enhancers based on the combination and selection of multiple features

Scientific Reports

Combines position-specific class profiles, nucleotide composition, and three-base sequence counts on the same historical benchmark. It predicts enhancer identity and strong/weak labels.

  • Supplied article PDF ↗
  • Corresponding benchmark and supplements →
Original materials · Prof. Dehzangi

Second baseline. Fit label-dependent profiles and feature selection only within training folds; its own feature-selection supplements are distinct from the supplied iEnhancer-2L supplements.

Read the source ↗
2018L03

Liu et al.

iEnhancer-EL: identifying enhancers and their strength with ensemble learning approach

Bioinformatics

Combines selected SVM predictors from 171 candidates. Uses the shared 2,968-sequence benchmark and a 400-sequence independent-test supplement.

  • Supplied article PDF ↗
  • Corresponding benchmark and supplements →
Original materials · Prof. Dehzangi

Later reconstruction. All 100 strong test sequences in the current publisher S4 match training sequences; removing them leaves no strong test class.

Read the source ↗
2023L04

Mir, Rehman, Tayara & Chong

Improving Enhancer Identification with a Multi-Classifier Stacked Ensemble Model

Journal of Molecular Biology

MCSE-Enhancer combines six sequence-descriptor families and multiple classifiers for enhancer identification. Its inspected public code uses the same 2,968 training and 400 test sequences as our files.

  • Supplied article PDF ↗
  • Corresponding benchmark and supplements →
  • Inspected author code and datasets ↗
Original materials · Prof. Dehzangi

Repair before use. The current notebooks contain incomplete cells, test-label optimization, and the same 100 overlaps. This does not establish the exact code used for the 2023 results.

Read the source ↗
2026P01

Moore, Pratt, Fan et al.

An expanded registry of candidate cis-regulatory elements

Nature

A reference map of candidate regulatory DNA. Our collection uses its human enhancer-like subset to annotate experimental regions.

Reference atlas

Candidate annotations are not cell-specific functional measurements.

Read the source ↗
2025P02

Agarwal, Inoue et al.

Massively parallel characterization of transcriptional regulatory elements

Nature

Large-scale lentiMPRA measurements in HepG2, K562, and WTC11, including a matched library designed with 60,000 constructs.

Experimental bridge

After reproducing the original method, compare its predictions with measured activity. Reproduce normalization first; old strong/weak annotations are not activity labels.

2025P03

Zhang et al.

Comprehensive evaluation of diverse massively parallel reporter assays to functionally characterize human enhancers genome-wide

Genome Biology

A comparison of six K562 datasets examining coverage, processing, orientation, and agreement among reporter assays.

Assay comparison

Reused source measurements must not be counted as independent validation.

Read in the order we plan to work

Start with iEnhancer-2L and its sequence/property supplements. Compare EnhancerPred before taking on the ensembles. Then read lentiMPRA for the experimental connection and the assay comparison for later validation. Numerical reproduction, cleaner evaluation, and transfer to new measurements are separate milestones.
